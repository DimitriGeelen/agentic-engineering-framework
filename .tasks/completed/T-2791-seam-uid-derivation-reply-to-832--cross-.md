## Context

832 shipped T-364 (RAIL-440): `aef:uid` is now **derived** (FNV-1a over the BPMN element
id) instead of minted from `Math.random()` at every parse. They flagged one seam question
back at us — *should they write `aef:uid` into third-party documents that never carried
one?* — and one transferable finding: **a probe run once against a probabilistic defect
cannot distinguish "the mechanism fires" from "the mechanism fires often enough."**

This task answers both with measurements over our corpus rather than agreement, and
captures the instrument-independence learning that surfaced while taking those
measurements.

## Acceptance Criteria

### Agent
- [x] Our corpus's `aef:uid` population is measured with the **correct byte shape**, and the
      measurement is reported as a population (files, elements, files-with-zero), not a
      single number. The first attempt matched `aef:uid=` and returned 0 of 1081 because the
      attribute is element syntax (`<aef:uid value="…"/>`) — a headline that was wrong by
      assuming a shape.
      → **51 files, 47 carrying uid, 1081 uid elements, 4 files with zero.** Values are
      semantic slugs (`ac_cron_fire`, `tl_work`); only 31 are `n_8hex`-shaped, and on
      inspection most of those are hand-authored fixture sequences or 832-origin bytes —
      exactly **one** (`t2584-scratch/v1.bpmn`, `n_44ecc63a`) is plausibly a minted value.
- [x] The claim "we are / are not exposed to the identity-permutation defect" is settled by
      checking the defect's **precondition** (a same-lane tie, which requires `aef:position`),
      not by observing that nothing has broken.
      → All 4 uid-less files have `aef:position=0`. Strictly-increasing x per lane, no tie can
      form. **Not exposed — by the same structural accident that protected 832's fixtures.**
- [x] Reply posted to the 832 DM rail that (a) answers the third-party-write question with a
      recommendation and its cost, (b) reports our uid vocabulary as an interop fact they
      cannot see from their side, and (c) states honestly whether their probe finding lands
      on our test suite — including if it does not.
      → Offset **441**, 7,704 bytes, read back intact. Recommendation: don't persist into
      foreign bytes — derivation removes the reason to, and persistence costs the
      open-and-save-no-op property they just restored; cost of that advice stated (only holds
      while derivation is a frozen contract). Probe finding: honest **no** for our test suite
      (our one atomicity probe is sequencing-atomic, hence deterministic), **yes** for our
      investigative probes, plus the positive/negative asymmetry as reciprocal contribution.
- [x] Learning captured for the cross-check-independence class: a two-way population check is
      only evidence if the two methods can fail **differently**. Ours agreed at diff=0 while
      both were corrupted identically by a shared normalisation step.
      → L-546.
