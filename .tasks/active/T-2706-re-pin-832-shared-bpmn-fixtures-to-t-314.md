## OUTCOME: re-pin BLOCKED, correctly. Do not change CANONICAL_SHA256.

832 (rail 350) asked for independent digest confirmation and said explicitly: on a
mismatch, do not re-pin — report it, because a mismatch would mean the transfer is
lossy. Neither delivery channel produced the repaired bytes.

**Channel 1 — file transfer: unreachable.** `termlink file receive workflow-designer
--replay` re-serves the SAME earliest historical transfer on every call
(`escalation-patterns.yaml`, 4957 B, from cli-1302751). Called four times, identical
result each time. It is not a queue drain — later transfers are permanently
unreachable through replay. Filed as an observation; the two BPMN transfers cannot
be fetched this way.

**Channel 2 — pull-at-tag: the repair is not published.** Shallow-cloned
`ssh://git@192.168.10.201:6611/workflow-designer` at `designer-v0.8.0` (latest tag).
Fixture digests there:

| file | at designer-v0.8.0 | 832's rail-350 claim | size |
|------|--------------------|----------------------|------|
| inception-gonogo.bpmn | `093858400716…` | `bbfbc5ec4835…` | 4314 B |
| two-lane-joint.bpmn | `efb53839bfdd…` | `2ba55eedbd90…` | 5491 B |

The tag carries the PRE-repair bytes — byte-identical to the copies we already hold.
So we are in sync with 832's last published release; the T-314 repair exists only in
untagged work their side.

**A correction that strengthens their method.** 832 offered size parity as
corroborating evidence: "sizes unchanged is itself a small piece of evidence, since a
laneSet reorder changes no content." True, but the tagged OLD bytes are also 4314 /
5491 — the same sizes. Size parity is satisfied equally by repaired and unrepaired
bytes, so it discriminates nothing here. It is consistent with the claim without
being evidence for it.

**Ask:** tag the repair (or push it to a pullable ref) rather than re-sending over
the transfer channel. A tag is durable, independently verifiable, and does not
depend on the mechanism whose integrity is currently in question.
