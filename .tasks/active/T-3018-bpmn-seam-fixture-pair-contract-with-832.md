## Recommendation

**Recommendation:** GO

**Rationale:**

The contract is the only proposed arrangement where neither side's checker is downstream of the thing it checks — each side's oracle comes from the other. Evidence is not hypothetical: 832 already shipped a silent escaping defect at this seam that their own DOMParser-based test could not see, and we hit the same lenient-reader class twice this week (file_state removal-detection, routing-mutation test). One confirmed silent defect at an untested seam is the failure-rate evidence. Recommend GO scoped to a 3-fixture adversarial pilot (unknown uid, double uid, uid on an element type our model cannot hold), NOT the N-comprehensive set — the boring cases pass on day one and teach nothing. Cost is bounded and the expected-rendering artifacts must be code-generated and committed, so drift shows as a review diff rather than a stale green.

**Evidence:**

- **The urgent question the contract was filed to answer is now answered — independently, and before any GO.** 832 built and measured the childless-`subProcess` third arm (arc offset 11934, their T-528). A `<bpmn:subProcess>` carrying only `extensionElements` round-trips as `{outcome: "survived", owner: "subProcess"}`, `children_after: 0` — **no retype, no injected placeholder child, uid and position intact.** Our collapsed-subProcess dialect is safe across this seam, which means the three published maps (`aef-task-lifecycle` et al.) are not at risk. Corroborated from their source independently of the measurement: their export tag map carries `// T-081: collapsed composite — BPMN-native tag, phase 1 emits no children`.
- **The arm was built to be able to fail, which is why the pass counts.** 832 mutated the export tag map for the exact failure I named — a "helpful cleanup" rewriting an empty scope element into a plain activity. One line, the kind of edit someone tidying node types could make without knowing our maps depend on it. Arm goes red, reports `survived-retyped`, and names what the node became; the flat arm (no subProcess at any level) stays clean, localising the red to the retype. 9/9 teeth.
- **Two errors 832 logged against their own expectations, both of which the instrument caught.** (1) They asserted the retyped owner would be `task`; it came back `serviceTask`, because their editor falls back rather than emitting the mutated tag verbatim. The arm reports *what the node became* rather than a boolean — had it returned `type preserved: false`, it would have confirmed a retype-to-`task` that never happened. (2) They asserted the nested arm would be unaffected by the retype mutant; it contains a subProcess of its own, so it obviously is. Both errors left in-source as comments rather than tidied away.
- **Failure-rate evidence for the seam is unchanged and still holds:** one confirmed silent escaping defect at this seam that 832's own DOMParser-based test could not see, plus two lenient-reader instances on our side in the same week (`file_state` removal-detection; routing-mutation test). T-3021 is a third of the same class.
- **What this does NOT cover, stated by 832 and carried here unchanged:** our reverse renderer. They can state what is in a document; they cannot state what we should produce from it. The fixture pair is still the only proposed arrangement where neither side's checker is downstream of the thing it checks.
- **Evidential-standing caveat 832 volunteered against their own past answers:** 6 of their 10 browser probes discard output on failure, and those six are the AEF-seam conformance probes specifically. They filed fixing that ahead of diagnosing their own flake rather than repairing it quietly. Every answer we have on this rail rests on an instrument that currently leaves nothing behind when it fails — which is an argument *for* the fixture pair, since committed expected-rendering artifacts survive a failed run by construction.

**What changed since filing:** the third arm exists and is pinned **regardless of this decision** — 832 built it under our "act on or ignore independently" arrangement. So a NO-GO no longer costs us the answer about our published maps; that is already banked. The decision is now narrower and less urgent than at filing: it is about **ongoing regression protection** at the seam, not about closing an open unknown. Recommendation stays GO on the same reasoning (the boring cases pass on day one and teach nothing; the 3-fixture adversarial pilot is the scoped version), but the operator should know the urgency has dropped.
