1. **Shared-account authorship leaves a verdict-authentication bypass.** R-43 permits an issue author’s GitHub “not fixed” reply to reopen it, but R-37 makes the secondary account the author of relayed issues. Every peer token therefore speaks as that author. This conflicts with §12.9 and leaves round-1 Codex finding 5 unresolved. R-61 also lacks explicit matching by issue, tested revision and test cycle. **Fix:** require sidecar confirmation for every secondary-account verdict, regardless of authorship; test spoofed replies and stale/replayed confirmations.

2. **R-52 would hide legitimate relay posts.** It applies the fixed-form verdict check to *every* secondary-account post, including the free-text relayed issues and recommendations R-37 requires that account to publish. Section 12.5 restricts trusted peers’ direct posts, not relay output. **Fix:** distinguish recorded relay publications from direct verdicts; scan both for disclosure, but apply the verdict-only schema to direct posts. Hiding through the operator’s account is a faithful implementation inference from §§12.3–12.5, rather than a new policy decision.

3. **The no-deeper-tests branch is an unconfirmed interpretation.** D-3 permits `UnderReview → Recommended` after a clean read when deeper tests are deemed unnecessary; R-22 and §10.31 adopt the same qualification. Section 12.6 lists deeper testing before merge without explicitly making it optional. Earlier risk-based depth supports the interpretation, but does not conclusively authorize skipping it. D-2 also lacks a corresponding skip edge. **Fix:** mark this branch provisional and obtain the operator’s decision on whether any raised risk level needs no deeper tests.

4. **The lifecycle still contradicts the revised testing order.** R-55 requires the pre-merge recommendation’s “field tests” to concern the PR head, while §5.17 and §12.6 place field tests after merge. D-3 routes AEF’s own fixes through FieldTest and labels FAILS as release-blocking, whereas §9.3.1.m says own fixes do not acquire that block. Asking the source to test an own fix is faithful to R-42; imposing a universal pre-release field-test stage is a new ordering decision, not established by “fixed or released.” **Fix:** use pre-merge test evidence in R-55; separate own-fix and outside-PR release transitions. Round-1 Codex finding 10 remains partly unresolved.

5. **Residual disclosure risk is understated.** R-29.e and §11.R.1 describe exposure as the interval before hiding, and describe fixed-form fields as narrowing what can leak. Yet they also acknowledge that token holders can publish arbitrary text. Hiding cannot retract copies already obtained; scanner downtime or missed classifications can prolong exposure. **Fix:** state that arbitrary public disclosure remains possible, potentially permanent, and distinguish cooperative schema compliance from protection against compromised tokens. Define scan coverage and outage behavior alongside §10.35 before requesting informed acceptance. Round-1 Codex/OpenCode finding 1 is only partly resolved.

6. **Detection promised for owner bypass exceeds the specified audit.** Section 11.R.5 includes direct pushes to `master` and claims every override is detected through R-65. R-65 audits merges into `bleeding-edge`; it does not cover direct `master` updates. R-6’s ancestry invariant also cannot distinguish an authorized release from an owner directly fast-forwarding `master` to an unreleased `bleeding-edge` commit. **Fix:** specify release-provenance auditing for `master`, or narrow the detection claim and leave that residual explicitly unresolved. Round-1 Codex finding 3 remains partly open.

7. **Some acceptance criteria still pass without satisfying their requirements.** R-7 tests only exclusion of nonpilot PRs and conflicts with R-12’s checks on every PR; an implementation that never processes Mehdi still passes. R-28’s eight-word rule permits copied short reports or text with every eighth word changed. R-17 still relies on dispatch records rather than demonstrating execution confinement. **Fix:** reconcile automated checks with pilot eligibility, add a successful pilot case, test rewrite provenance rather than word overlap alone, and verify prohibited execution attempts. Round-1 Codex finding 9 and OpenCode finding 7(a) remain incomplete; card 6.2 is not substantively satisfied.

8. **The isolation floor contains an undefined exception.** R-53 allows network paths to estate hosts “beyond what the test needs,” while its rationale says test hosts must be unable to reach those hosts and its test expects every attempted connection to fail. **Fix:** remove the exception or explicitly identify permitted destinations and leave their approval open. This weakens the boundary intended to resolve Codex finding 2/OpenCode finding 4.

9. **Earlier-proposal disposition remains inaccurate.** Section 8.1 marks branching from `bleeding-edge` “kept,” but a PR’s target does not establish where its branch originated; §12.2 expressly repeats that proposal. **Fix:** state the branch-origin requirement or record an explicit unresolved disposition. Codex finding 11 remains partly unresolved. Card 6.4’s sections exist, and “impossible” requirements name adversaries, but card 6.5’s diagram/text consistency fails on the lifecycle differences above; rendering success alone does not resolve them.

Verdict: **amber — fixes first**; the binding decisions are substantially incorporated, but authentication, scan scope, testing transitions and residual-risk claims still prevent informed sign-off.
