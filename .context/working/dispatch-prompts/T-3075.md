# What makes this review worth their time

They can grade their own dossier for internal consistency. What they cannot do is
check it against what this substrate *actually does* — which is frequently not
what our own CLAUDE.md reads like it does. Two classes of finding:

- **Class 1 — the dossier is factually wrong about AEF today.** It claims a
  primitive exists, or works differently than it does. Cheap to find, cheap to fix,
  low value.
- **Class 2 — the dossier is right about AEF today, and that is the problem.** It
  builds on a primitive that exists but is thinner, blinder, or more advisory than
  its name suggests. This is the class worth writing.

Weight the review toward Class 2. Say which class each finding is.

# Evidence discipline — the part that must not slip

Every claim you make about AEF is either **observed** (cite `path:line`, or the
verbatim output of a command you ran) or **inference** (say so). Do not present a
structural proxy plus an inference as a measurement (L-589). Do not cite
`CLAUDE.md` as evidence that a gate behaves a certain way — CLAUDE.md is the
*description*; the hook, the lib function, and the test are the *behaviour*, and
this repo has repeatedly found the two diverged.

Where you cannot verify something in the time you have, write "unverified" and say
what would settle it. An honest gap is worth more to them than a confident guess.
